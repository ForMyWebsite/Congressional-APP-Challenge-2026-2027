# Student usability-testing guide

This is a proposed test, not completed research. Do not record results until real sessions occur. Use invented names and the fictional Demo studio; avoid collecting student records. Arrange appropriate adult/school consent for student ages and local requirements.

## 20-minute session

1. Explain that the design is being tested, not the student. Participation is optional; stop at any time.
2. Ask the student to think aloud while creating a profile. Can they skip an answer, review it and understand where it is stored?
3. Scenario: “You’re curious about engineering, prefer free options, and have schoolwork tomorrow. Find a starting point and check what you know and don't know.”
4. Ask them to distinguish the real unreviewed links from the fictional engineering example. Ask whether “free participation” includes a supplied device, whether an unknown deadline means no deadline, and whether an interest match guarantees eligibility.
5. Save the example. Ask them to identify a suggestion, a question and a sample requirement. Ask what saving and application status actually mean.
6. Add an assignment; choose preparation time/days and a target; preview sessions. Can they explain a conflict, identify an unscheduled step, and approve deliberately?
7. Complete a prerequisite and dependent step, then reopen the prerequisite. Ask what changed and why.
8. Reload, find progress, export JSON/ICS, and identify deletion and storage limits.

## Observe without leading

Record completion/not-completed, where help was needed, unclear words, mistaken assumptions, and a brief anonymized quote only with consent. Ask “What do you expect this will do?” before a consequential control. Test keyboard-only interaction and small screens. Do not infer disability, income, ability or disadvantage.

Prioritize confusion that could cause false eligibility, a missed deadline, lost data or an unintended schedule change. Report observed sessions and actual counts separately from suggestions; don't invent impact metrics or claim these proposed tests were completed.
